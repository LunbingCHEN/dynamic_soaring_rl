Learning step-level dynamic soaring in shear flow

Dynamic soaring enables seabirds to travel thousands of kilometers by continuously extracting energy from atmospheric wind shear. This project asks whether the same energy-harvesting flight can emerge from step-level state feedback based only on local sensing, without explicit trajectory planning.

Lunbing Chen, Jixin Lu, Yufei Yin, Jinpeng Huang, Yong Chen, Yang Xiang, and Hong Liu

Manuscript under second-round revision at Nature Communications.

Overview

Existing theoretical and computational studies predominantly describe dynamic soaring at the level of complete cycles, implicitly assuming that the flow remains stable over each maneuver. In realistic near-surface environments, wind fields vary on spatial and temporal scales comparable to a single maneuver, so predefined cyclic strategies may become suboptimal or dynamically infeasible.

Is explicit cycle-level global planning necessary, or can sustained energy extraction and navigation emerge from step-level feedback based solely on local sensing?

A step-level description therefore requires instantaneous local decisions to achieve the global objectives of energy extraction and long-range navigation in a three-dimensional, spatially varying flow field.

  1. Step-level feedback. Dynamic soaring emerges from state-feedback control using only local sensing, without prescribing a full soaring cycle.
  2. Robust navigation. The learned policies support omnidirectional navigation across diverse shear-flow conditions, including spatially varying winds.
  3. Structured control law. Local sensing and feedback organize into an interpretable strategy that produces global, long-distance navigation.

Method and results

We formulate dynamic soaring as a continuous feedback-navigation problem with coupled objectives of atmospheric-energy extraction and directional progress. Deep reinforcement learning is used as a tool to identify a step-level control policy from instantaneous aircraft and flow states.

A Soft Actor-Critic policy is trained through curriculum learning across wind conditions and target directions. Observation and reward ablations then connect the learned decisions to flight dynamics, sensing requirements, and energy management.

  • Sustained energy-harvesting flight across wind-relative target directions.
  • Emergent switching between energy harvesting and target-directed gliding.
  • Adaptation to changing winds and moving targets using local observations.
  • Generalization beyond the uniform shear flows used during training.

Connection to autonomous UAV flight

The step-level policy provides the decision-making foundation for a Titan Heron-based autonomous dynamic-soaring UAV. The implementation integrates onboard state and wind sensing, DRL policy deployment, and real-time PX4 control.

This work connects the original scientific problem—how a flyer extracts energy from an environmental flow—to closed-loop fixed-wing implementation and outdoor flight testing.
